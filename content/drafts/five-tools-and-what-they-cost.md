There are a thousand "10 best free AI tools" lists. Almost all of them are
copied from each other, and almost none of them tell you the thing that
actually matters: **what happens when the free part runs out.**

One of the tools below gives you 400 credits *once* — not per month — and most
people burn the lot in their first sitting without realising there is a floor
under them. Another one is free but **legally cannot be used for your business.**

So this list is arranged differently. Each tool gets three things: what problem
it actually solves, what the surprise is, and **what it costs you.** That last
line is the reason to read this instead of the other lists.

Checked August 2026.

## 1. NotebookLM — turns your own documents into a podcast

**The problem it solves:** you have two hundred pages you are supposed to
understand and no time to read them. A syllabus. A policy document. A tender. A
contract. Six research papers for one assignment.

You upload the files. It generates a **ten to twenty minute conversation between
two hosts** who explain *your* material to each other — asking each other
questions, disagreeing, working through the hard parts.

You can interrupt them and ask your own question in the middle.

### Why this is the surprising one

People expect a summary. A summary is a shorter version of a thing you still
have to read.

This is different. It is your own textbook, in your ears, on the train. The
material did not get shorter — it got *listenable*. That is a different kind of
useful, and it is the reason this is first on the list.

### What it costs you

**Genuinely free, and genuinely generous.** 100 notebooks, 50 sources in each,
50 questions a day, three audio overviews a day. No card required.

This is the only tool on this page with no meaningful catch. If you try one
thing from this post, make it this.

**Best for:** students, and anyone handed a long document they did not ask for.

## 2. Napkin — turns a paragraph into a diagram

**The problem it solves:** you can explain your process perfectly out loud, but
you cannot draw it. So the explanation stays as a wall of text that nobody
reads.

Paste what you wrote. It produces flowcharts, timelines, comparison tables,
process diagrams — all editable, exporting to PNG and PDF.

### Why it is surprising

It is not clipart, and it is not a template you fill in.

It read your paragraph and worked out **what shape the idea is** — that this
bit is a sequence, that bit is a comparison, this other bit is a hierarchy.
Getting the shape right is the part people find hard, and it is the part it
does for you.

### What it costs you

Free tier gives unlimited basic visuals and unlimited PNG and PDF export, with
weekly credits for the AI generation — but **a watermark on everything.**

Removing the watermark is around **$18 a month**, which is real money in rupees.
So: free is fine for your own notes and internal documents. For a client
deliverable, you are paying.

**Best for:** freelancers, teachers, anyone who has to explain something to
someone who will not read four paragraphs.

## 3. HeyGen Video Translate — your video, your face, speaking Hindi

**The problem it solves:** you recorded something in English, and half the
people you want to reach would rather hear it in Hindi or Marathi.

Upload the video. It re-voices you in another language **and moves your lips to
match.** Over 175 languages.

### Why this is the "wait, what?" one

This is the biggest reaction on the page, and it is worth being precise about
why.

It is not subtitles — nobody reads subtitles on a phone. It is not a robot
voiceover dubbed on top. It is **your own face, speaking a language you do not
speak,** with your mouth moving correctly.

For anyone making content for a mixed-language audience — which in Mumbai is
everyone — that is a genuinely new thing to be able to do.

### What it costs you

Read this carefully, because it is the tightest limit on the page.

The free plan is **three videos a month, three minutes each, 720p, with a
watermark on every export.**

That is a demo allowance, not a workflow. Treat it as "see the thing work once
and understand what is now possible" — not as something you run a channel on.
If you want to use it properly, it is a paid tool.

**Best for:** anyone making content for people who do not share their first
language.

## 4. ElevenLabs Scribe — Hindi speech to text that is actually accurate

**The problem it solves:** typing in English is slow, and typing in Hindi is
worse. Speaking is faster for most people — but transcription has historically
been bad at Indian languages, so nobody trusted it.

Record or upload. It transcribes. On the FLEURS benchmark it reports a **3.1%
word error rate**, ahead of both Google's Gemini and OpenAI's Whisper.

### Why it belongs here

Nobody's jaw drops at this one. That is fine — it is the tool on this list most
likely to still be in use in six months.

For someone who thinks in Hindi and has to produce English text all day, it
removes a small friction that repeats twenty times a day. Those are the ones
that actually change how you work.

### What it costs you

The free tier is 10,000 credits a month — roughly **ten minutes** of the
text-to-speech side.

And the part that catches people: **the free plan carries no commercial
rights.** You must credit ElevenLabs, and you cannot legally monetise anything
made with it. Fine for personal use and for testing. **Not** for your shop's
advertisement or a client's video.

If you only need long transcripts, **TurboScribe** gives three free a day and
handles files up to ten hours.

**Best for:** anyone who would rather talk than type.

## 5. Gamma — a full presentation from one sentence

**The problem it solves:** you need slides by tomorrow morning and you have not
started.

Describe the topic. It produces a designed deck — layout, images, structure,
the lot — in about a minute.

### Why it is satisfying

Going from an empty screen to something that looks finished is a real feeling,
whether it is a college presentation or a client pitch. The blank page is the
hard part, and this removes it.

### What it costs you — this is the one to read twice

Every list calls Gamma free. It is not, quite.

**The free plan gives 400 credits once. Not monthly. Once.**

- Generating a deck: **40 credits**
- Each AI image: **10 credits**
- Each extra card: **5 credits**

Do the arithmetic and you get somewhere between three and ten presentations,
total, forever. Most people burn the whole allowance exploring in their first
sitting and then hit a wall halfway through the deck that actually mattered.

Free decks also carry Gamma branding on them.

So the advice is specific: **do not explore with it.** Use it for the one
presentation that genuinely matters, know the number before you start, and
accept that it is a paid tool after that.

**Best for:** students and anyone pitching. Once, deliberately.

## What to do this week

Again — pick one, not five. A list of five gets saved and never opened.

If you are studying, or you have a long document you have been avoiding:
**NotebookLM.** It costs nothing, it has no trap in it, and it is the one most
likely to change a habit rather than entertain you for ten minutes.

If you make videos in English for people who would rather hear Hindi: spend one
of your three free HeyGen videos on a real clip. Not to publish it — just to see
your own face say something you never said.

That is usually the moment the whole thing stops being abstract.
