## 1. WhatsApp can answer your customers for you. Free.

**This is the biggest one on the page, and almost nobody in India is using it.**

In May 2026, Meta switched on **Business AI** inside the WhatsApp Business app
for Indian small businesses. You give it your product catalogue and your
business documents. It then answers customer questions on its own — prices,
what is in stock, delivery, discounts — books appointments, and collects leads.

All day. All night. In every native Indian language.

### Why this matters more than any tool on any list

Think about where your business actually happens. It is not on a website. It is
not in an app you downloaded last month. It is in WhatsApp, on the same phone
you are reading this on, and it has been there for years.

Every "AI for small business" article tells a shop owner to go and learn a new
platform. Meanwhile the thing is sitting in a tab inside the app they already
have open sixteen hours a day.

The part that makes this trustworthy: **it answers from your catalogue and your
documents, not from the internet.** It is not guessing your prices. If you did
not tell it something, it does not know it.

### Where to find it

Open the **WhatsApp Business** app — the one with the green shopfront icon, not
normal WhatsApp. Then:

1. Tap the **Tools** tab
2. Tap **Your Business AI**
3. Follow the setup — it asks you questions, you answer them
4. Upload your catalogue and any documents about your business

No coding. No third-party service. No separate subscription.

### The catch

The rollout is staged and there are eligibility criteria, so **not every
business will see the tab yet.** If it is not there, you have not done anything
wrong — check again in a few weeks.

And test it on yourself before you let it face customers. Message your own
business and see what it says. If it gets a price wrong, that is a promise you
have to keep.

## 2. Google Photos can be searched in plain Hindi

You have eleven thousand photos and you find things by scrolling for five
minutes with your thumb.

**Ask Photos** lets you just describe what you want:

- *"the photo of my bike's number plate"*
- *"the receipt from the electrician"*
- *"what did I eat in Goa"*

It is live in India, and it understands **Hindi, Marathi, Bengali, Tamil,
Telugu and Gujarati** — so you can ask in the language you actually think in,
instead of translating in your head first.

The same feature edits by description too. Rather than hunting for the right
slider, you tell it what to change.

### Where to find it

Google Photos app, then the **Ask** tab at the bottom. Type it or say it.

### The catch — read this one

It only works on photos **backed up to Google Photos**, which means those photos
sit on Google's servers, not only on your phone.

For holiday pictures, fine. For photographs of your Aadhaar card, your bank
passbook, your signed cheques — think about that properly first. Most people
have all three somewhere in their camera roll and have never once considered
where they live.

That is not a reason to avoid the feature. It is a reason to know what you are
agreeing to.

## 3. Circle anything on your screen to understand it

Hold the home button, **circle anything on the screen with your finger**, and
get an explanation without leaving the app you are in.

A clause in a rental agreement. A part in a photo of a machine. A maths problem
in a PDF. A medicine name on a strip.

This is **Circle to Search**, and it is on most Android phones from Android 12
onwards. Most people who have it have never used it once.

Google Lens also has a **Homework mode** that shows the *steps* rather than just
the answer — algebra, geometry, calculus. And in August 2026 Google added
**practice quizzes covering JEE and NEET** to Search's AI Mode, free and
worldwide.

### Why this is underrated

Parents pay real money every month for tutoring apps that do exactly this. A
free version is built into the phone already in the child's hand.

### The catch

It explains steps well and **it still gets things wrong.** Confidently.

Treat it as a study aid, not an answer key. A student who copies answers without
following the steps will eventually copy a wrong one into an exam.

## 4. Indian college students: one free year of Google AI Plus

Not a hack. A deadline.

Google is giving **eligible Indian college students a free year of Google AI
Plus** — normally around **₹4,000**. It includes higher usage limits on Gemini
and 400 GB of storage.

**Where:** gemini.google.com/students
**You need:** student verification
**Deadline: 31 December 2026**

If you are studying, or there is a student in the house, do this one today
rather than bookmarking this page. Offers like this end quietly and nobody sends
a reminder.

## 5. Your keyboard can fix your English before you send it

The quiet one on the list, and probably the one that gets used most.

**Gboard Writing Tools** proofreads and rewrites text inside the keyboard
itself, in any app. Change the tone to formal, friendly or concise. Fix grammar
and spelling in one tap. It works in WhatsApp, in email, in a form — anywhere
you can type.

And it runs **on the device.** Your message never leaves your phone to be
checked. That is a meaningfully better privacy position than any writing app you
have to paste your text into.

### Why it matters more than it sounds

If you think in Hindi and have to write to a client in English, there is a small
tax you pay on every message — the re-reading, the second-guessing, the "does
this sound rude?" This removes it, inside the app you were already in.

### The catch

It needs Gemini Nano, so it only runs on newer high-end phones — Pixel 9 and 10,
and selected Samsung, OnePlus and Xiaomi models. **Many people reading this will
not have it,** and there is no way to add it.

Check your keyboard settings. If it is not there, it is the phone, not you.

## Now the part nobody writes: what is NOT free

Three features you will read about everywhere, described as though anyone can
use them. You cannot.

| Feature | What people say | What is actually true |
|---|---|---|
| **The AI formula in Google Sheets** | "Write one formula, AI runs on every row" | Real, and genuinely powerful. **But it needs a paid Workspace plan or Google One AI Premium.** Not available on a free Gmail account. |
| **Google Meet "Take notes for me"** | "Free automatic meeting notes" | **Paid only** — Workspace Business Standard and above, or Google AI Pro. Free Gmail does not have it. |
| **Meta AI inside WhatsApp** | "Unlimited free AI in your chats" | Still free, but **Meta cut the limits in June 2026** — from 200 to 50 prompts a day for logged-in users, and 25 for logged-out. |

I am including this because it is the part that always gets left out, and
leaving it out is how people end up on a subscription they did not mean to
start.

**And one more, which is costing people money right now.** In July 2025 Airtel
gave its customers a free 12-month Perplexity Pro subscription worth around
₹17,000. Enormous numbers of people in India claimed it. New claims closed in
January 2026 — which means **the earliest free years began expiring in the
middle of 2026, rolling into paid auto-renewal unless the person switched it
off.**

If you claimed that offer, go and check your renewal setting now.

Pick one. Not five.

That last one takes about eleven seconds, and is usually the moment people
