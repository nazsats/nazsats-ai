I build software for a living, and I use AI every day. So the thing I keep
noticing is not about the technology at all.

It is that almost everyone I know outside of tech has already tried it — opened
ChatGPT once, typed something in, got back a wall of text that sounded like a
press release, and quietly decided it was not for them.

They were not wrong about what they saw. They were wrong about why.

## What actually happened

Nobody showed them. That is it. That is the entire gap.

There is no shortage of writing about AI, but nearly all of it is aimed at
people who already work in tech. It assumes you know what an API is, that you
enjoy trying new tools on a Sunday, and that you have time to experiment.

Most people have none of those things. They have a job, a family, and about ten
minutes.

Meanwhile the people who did learn it are pulling ahead — not because they are
smarter, but because someone happened to show them a trick early. That is a
very ordinary kind of unfairness, and it is easy to fix.

## Who I am writing for

Three groups, because their problems are genuinely different:

- **Students and job seekers.** You are applying to a hundred places and writing
  each application by hand.
- **Small business owners.** You spend two hours a day answering the same
  WhatsApp questions and doing paperwork instead of the work you started the
  business to do.
- **Freelancers.** You are one person trying to deliver like three.

## What I will send you

One short lesson, a few times a week. Each one is a single thing you can use the
same day.

Every lesson shows the **actual words to type**. Not "be more specific" — the
literal prompt, before and after, so you can copy it and see the difference
yourself. That is the part most guides skip, and it is the only part that
matters.

## What I will not do

I will not tell you about a new tool every week. Learning a new app every seven
days is not progress, it is a treadmill — and it teaches you to wait for the
next thing instead of using what you already have. Almost everything here works
with the free version of tools you can open right now.

I will not pretend AI is magic. It is confidently wrong on a regular basis, it
should never see your bank details or your medical reports, and there are things
you should keep doing yourself. I will tell you when.

And I am not selling you a course.

## Why I am doing it

I got to learn this because of the job I happen to have. That is luck, not
merit. The least I can do is pass it on to the people who did not get that
particular piece of luck.

If that sounds useful, stay. If not, leaving costs you nothing — and I would
rather you leave than mute me.
