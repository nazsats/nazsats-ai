# Consulting — what to offer, and what to learn first

You asked what consulting you could do and what you should learn. Here is the
honest version, ordered by how quickly each one can actually earn.

## The thing to understand first

Nobody pays for "AI training". They pay to stop losing time or money on a
specific problem they can name. "I spend two hours a day answering the same
WhatsApp questions" is a thing people pay to fix. "Learn AI" is not.

So every offer below is framed as a problem, not a technology.

---

## Five offers, easiest to hardest

### 1. The AI hour — ₹1,500–3,000

One hour, one person, screen share. They bring a real task; you do it together
with them driving.

**Why start here.** No preparation, no materials, no risk. You find out what
people actually struggle with — which becomes your content, your workshop, and
your case studies. Ten of these will teach you more about your market than any
amount of planning.

Do the first three free in exchange for a written testimonial and permission to
use it as an anonymised example.

### 2. The workflow audit — ₹5,000–15,000

Half a day with a small business. You watch how they work, then deliver a short
document: the three things worth automating, what to use, and roughly what each
saves.

**You are selling the diagnosis, not the fix.** That is a real service, and it
is also the natural lead-in to being paid to implement it.

### 3. The workshop — ₹15,000–40,000 per session

Two to three hours for a team of 10–25. Same material each time, which is what
makes it profitable: build it once, deliver it many times.

Sell to: co-working spaces, small agencies, colleges, business associations,
local chambers of commerce. Colleges pay less but fill rooms and generate
enormous reach.

### 4. Done-for-you setup — ₹20,000–60,000

They do not want to learn it. They want it working. You set up their templates,
their reusable prompts, an automation or two, and train one person to maintain
it.

**Higher value, and the hardest to leave.** Also the one that risks becoming
unpaid ongoing support — say clearly what is included and for how long.

### 5. Retainer — ₹15,000–30,000/month

A few hours a month, ongoing. Only ever grows out of one of the above. Never
sell it first.

> Ranges are starting points for Mumbai, not fixed prices. The right move is to
> quote, watch the reaction, and adjust. If nobody ever hesitates, you are too
> cheap.

## What to learn, in order

You already have the technical half. This is the missing half.

**1. Running a discovery call.** The whole skill is asking "what does that cost
you today?" and then being quiet. Most people talk themselves into the sale if
you let them. This is the highest-leverage thing on this list.

**2. Scoping and saying no.** Write down what is included, what is not, and how
many revisions. Every horror story in consulting is a scoping failure.

**3. Facilitation.** Teaching 20 people is a different skill from explaining to
one. Learn to handle the person who dominates the room, the silence after you
ask a question, and the one who says "AI will take our jobs" — because someone
always does.

**4. Case studies.** "Saved a bakery six hours a week on customer messages" sells
more than any credential. Ask permission at the end of every engagement, while
they are pleased with you.

**5. Basic accounting.** GST registration, invoices, what to set aside for tax.
Dull, and it becomes urgent at exactly the wrong moment.

## How the site feeds this

The lessons are the proof. Somebody reads three, decides you explain things
clearly, and then wants you in their office.

That path needs to exist, so:

- Add a `/work-with-me` page once you have ~10 lessons published
- One line at the end of business-focused lessons: *"I do this with businesses
  in Mumbai — reply if you want a hand."*
- Nothing pushier than that. The content is the pitch; a hard sell on a free
  lesson undoes it.

## The realistic timeline

- **Month 1–2:** publish consistently, grow the group, do free AI hours
- **Month 3:** first paid one-to-one, first case study
- **Month 4–6:** first workshop, priced from what you learned in the audits
- **After that:** repeat the workshop, and let inbound decide the rest

The slow part is trust, and there is no way to buy it. That is precisely why
publishing three times a week beats anything clever.
