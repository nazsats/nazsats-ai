# Content plan

Thirty lessons, grouped so you always know what to write next. Each is one idea
someone can use the same day.

## The format that works

Every lesson, same shape:

1. **The mistake** — what most people do (they recognise themselves)
2. **Why it fails** — the actual reason, in one paragraph
3. **The fix** — with a real before/after they can copy
4. **Try it now** — one concrete thing to do in the next ten minutes

Keep it under 500 words. The people you are writing for are reading on a phone
between other tasks. A long post is not more generous, it is just less likely to
be finished.

## Rules worth keeping

- **One idea per lesson.** If it needs two, it is two lessons — and you need
  thirty of them.
- **Show the actual prompt.** Not "be more specific" — the literal text, before
  and after. This is the single biggest thing that separates useful from
  forgettable.
- **No tool of the week.** Recommending a new app every post trains people to
  wait for the next one instead of using what they have. Stick to what is free
  and available.
- **Indian context.** UPI, WhatsApp, GST, local business examples. Generic
  American examples quietly tell readers this was not written for them.
- **Never open with "In today's fast-paced world".** It reads as AI-written,
  which is fatal for a person teaching AI.

## Batch 1 — Prompts (start here)

The highest-value topic, and the one nobody teaches properly.

1. ~~Stop asking AI to "write something"~~ ✅ *written*
2. **The three-part prompt** — who you are, who it's for, what good looks like
3. **Ask for options, not answers** — "give me 5 versions, short" beats one reply
4. **Tell it what you don't want** — negative instructions are underused
5. **Paste your own writing in** — the fastest way to stop sounding like a robot
6. **When it's wrong, don't start over** — say what's wrong and ask for a fix
7. **The "explain it to me like I'm not in this field" trick**

## Batch 2 — Students & job seekers

Biggest audience, most shareable, least able to pay — treat it as reach.

8. **Your CV is not a list of duties** — turning tasks into outcomes with AI
9. **Tailoring one CV to ten jobs in twenty minutes**
10. **Interview practice with AI** — the prompt that makes it ask hard questions
11. **Understanding a job description** — what they're really asking for
12. **Writing a cover letter that isn't obviously AI**
13. **Learning a topic fast** — the Feynman method with a machine
14. **Checking your own work** — asking AI to argue against you

## Batch 3 — Small business owners

Where the money is. Every lesson should save an hour or make a sale.

15. **Customer replies in your own voice** — build a reusable prompt once
16. **Product descriptions that don't sound copy-pasted**
17. **Turning one photo into a week of posts**
18. **Handling a bad review** — draft, cool down, send
19. **Summarising your own WhatsApp order chaos**
20. **Writing quotes and invoices faster**
21. **Reading a contract before you sign it** — with a large warning that this
    is not legal advice

## Batch 4 — Freelancers

Most likely to pay for something later.

22. **Scoping a project properly** — the questions to ask before quoting
23. **Saying no to bad clients, politely**
24. **Turning delivered work into a case study**
25. **Doubling your output without hiring**
26. **Pricing conversations** — practising the awkward part

## Batch 5 — Judgement

Establishes that you're honest, not a hype merchant. This is what earns trust.

27. **When AI is confidently wrong** — how to spot it
28. **What you should never paste into a chatbot** — bank details, Aadhaar,
    customer data, medical reports
29. **AI cannot do your thinking** — the tasks to keep for yourself
30. **Free vs paid** — an honest answer to the question everyone asks

## Cadence

**Three lessons a week is enough**, and it is sustainable for a year. Two a week
is fine. Seven a week for a month and then nothing is the pattern that kills
most of these — the audience learns you are unreliable and stops opening.

Write three at a time in one sitting. Publishing is then a two-minute job, and
you are never writing under pressure.

## Where each one goes

| Where | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Site** | The full lesson | Permanent, searchable, links well |
| **WhatsApp** | Two-line summary + link | Chat is for the hook, not the essay |
| **LinkedIn** | Rewritten as a post, no link in body | LinkedIn suppresses posts with outbound links — put it in the first comment |

Write it once for the site. The other two are edits, not new work.
