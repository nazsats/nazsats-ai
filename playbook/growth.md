# Growing the WhatsApp community and LinkedIn group

Two very different places. The most common mistake is posting the same thing to
both.

---

## WhatsApp

### Community, not group

Use a **WhatsApp Community with an announcement channel**, not a normal group.

In a normal group of 200 people, anyone can post, someone shares a good-morning
image at 6am, and the useful members mute it within a week. Muted is dead.

A Community gives you a broadcast-only announcement channel plus optional
discussion groups underneath. You control the signal; conversation is opt-in.

### Cadence

Three posts a week, same days, roughly the same time. Predictability is what
turns a channel into a habit.

**Best times for Indian audiences:** 8–9am (commute) or 8–10pm (after dinner).
Avoid 1–3pm entirely.

### What a post looks like

Two lines, then the link. Not the essay.

> Most people ask AI to "write something" and get back a bank letter.
> One extra sentence fixes it — here's the exact prompt.
> nazsatsai.com/lessons/stop-asking-ai-to-write-something

The two lines have to work alone. Many people will never tap the link, and they
should still get something — that is what makes them keep you unmuted.

### Growing it

- **Your existing contacts first.** Personal messages, not a broadcast. "I've
  started writing short AI tips for non-technical people — want me to add you?"
  Twenty of these beats any growth tactic.
- **One shareable lesson a month.** Something a person forwards to their family
  group. The CV and interview ones travel furthest.
- **Ask directly, once you have ~50 members.** "If this was useful, forward it
  to one person who'd like it." People do it when asked and rarely otherwise.
- **Never buy members or bulk-add.** WhatsApp bans for it, and you lose the
  number that everything is attached to.

### The rule that keeps it alive

Every post must be useful without clicking anything. The moment the channel
becomes a list of links, it becomes marketing, and people leave.

## LinkedIn

Different psychology. WhatsApp is people who already know you. LinkedIn is
strangers deciding whether you are worth following — and it is where the
consulting clients are.

### Post, don't link

LinkedIn suppresses posts containing outbound links. Write the lesson **as the
post itself**, then put the link in the first comment.

That feels wasteful. It is not — the post is the reach, the site is the archive.

### The shape that works

```
A hook that is one line and slightly uncomfortable.

The mistake, stated plainly.

Why it happens.

The fix — with the actual before/after text.

One question to the reader.
```

Short paragraphs. One line each, mostly. LinkedIn is read on a phone in a lift.

### Cadence

Two or three a week. More than that and reach per post drops.

**Best times:** Tuesday–Thursday, 8–10am IST.

### Groups vs your own feed

Your **own feed** is worth more than any group. LinkedIn groups are mostly dead;
posts there reach very few people.

Better: post on your profile, and comment thoughtfully on posts by people your
audience already follows. Ten good comments a week will do more for reach than
ten group posts.

### What converts to consulting

Not the tips. The tips build trust; **the specifics sell**.

Every few weeks, post something with a real number in it:

> A bakery in Santacruz was spending 2 hours a day on WhatsApp order queries.
> We built one reusable prompt. It's now about 20 minutes.
> Here's the prompt.

That is the post that produces a DM asking whether you do this for others.

---

## Measuring it

Ignore follower count. Watch:

| Signal | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Forwards / reposts** | The only real measure of whether it was useful |
| **Replies and DMs** | Where consulting comes from |
| **Site visits from WhatsApp** | Whether the two-line hook is working |
| **Members who never leave** | Retention beats growth every time |

A channel of 200 people who read everything is worth vastly more than 2,000 who
have muted you.

---

## The first month

**Week 1** — Set up the Community. Personally invite 20 people. Publish 3 lessons.
**Week 2** — 3 more lessons. Start posting on LinkedIn. Comment daily.
**Week 3** — Ask members to forward one lesson. Offer 3 free AI hours.
**Week 4** — Write up what you learned in those calls as a lesson. Repeat.

The loop is: teach → hear the real problems → teach that → someone asks for
help → that becomes a case study → which brings more people.

Everything compounds. Nothing works in week one.
