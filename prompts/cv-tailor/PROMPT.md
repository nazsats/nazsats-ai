PART 1 — HONEST MATCH SCORE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Score how well my CV matches this job, 0-100. Show it as:

MATCH: XX/100

Then break it down:

- **Hard requirements met** — list each must-have from the job description and
  whether my CV actually evidences it. Quote the line from my CV that proves it.
- **Hard requirements missing** — the ones nothing in my CV supports. Be blunt.
  This is the most useful part of your answer.
  - If a requirement is about years of experience and my CV gives dates but no

- **Nice-to-haves met** — same treatment, briefly.

Then one line: **Worth applying?** — yes, yes-with-caveats, or probably not,

Scoring guide, so the number means something:
- 85-100: you meet every hard requirement and most nice-to-haves
- 70-84: you meet every hard requirement, gaps only in nice-to-haves
- 50-69: you miss one hard requirement but have adjacent evidence
- 30-49: you miss several hard requirements
- 0-29: different field or level entirely

PART 2 — TAILORED CV
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Now rewrite my CV for this specific job.

────────── ABSOLUTE RULES — DO NOT BREAK THESE ──────────

1. **Invent nothing.** Every skill, tool, employer, job title, date, metric,
   qualification and achievement in your output must already appear in my CV. If
   it is not in my CV, it does not go in the rewrite. No exceptions.

2. **Never inflate — and never shrink.** Do not change dates, durations, years
   of experience, team sizes, or numbers. If my CV says 2 years, it says 2 years.

   **Do not calculate durations yourself.** You do not reliably know today's
   date, so you cannot work out how long "2023 – present" has been. Use whatever
   duration my CV already states. If my CV states none and the role is ongoing,
   write the dates as they appear and say nothing about total years. Guessing
   here understates real experience, which costs me interviews just as surely as
   exaggerating would.

3. **Never claim a skill I do not have.** If the job wants Kubernetes and my CV
   has no Kubernetes, the rewrite has no Kubernetes. Do not imply it. Do not
   write "container orchestration" as a way of sneaking it in.

4. **Adjacent is not equal.** If I know MySQL and they want PostgreSQL, you may
   write "relational databases (MySQL)" — accurate and findable. You may not
   write "PostgreSQL" or "SQL databases including PostgreSQL".

5. If I am missing something important, say so in Part 1 and leave it out of
   Part 2. **A CV that gets me into an interview I cannot survive is worse than
   no interview.**

─────────────── WHAT YOU SHOULD DO ───────────────

Within those rules, tailor aggressively:

- **Reorder.** Put the experience this job cares about first. Bury what it does

- **Expand what is relevant, compress what is not.** A bullet they care about

- **Surface buried evidence.** If something in a side project or education
  section meets a requirement, promote it.
- **Lead with outcomes** where my CV already contains them. Do not manufacture
  numbers that are not there.
- **Keep it truthful and boring rather than impressive and false.**

**WHAT I DELIBERATELY DID NOT ADD** — every requirement from the job description
