PART 2 — THE TAILORED CV
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Rewrite my CV for this job, section by section, in the same order my CV already

────────── NEVER BREAK THESE ──────────

1. **Invent nothing.** Every skill, tool, employer, job title, date, number and
   qualification must already appear in my CV. If it is not there, it does not
   go in.

2. **Never inflate, never shrink.** Do not change dates, durations, team sizes
   or metrics. Do not calculate how long "to present" has been.

3. **Never claim a skill I do not have.** If the job wants Kubernetes and my CV
   has none, the rewrite has none. Do not imply it with a vaguer phrase.

4. **Adjacent is not equal.** If I know MySQL and they want PostgreSQL, write
   "relational databases (MySQL)" — true and findable. Do not write PostgreSQL.

5. **A listed skill is not an achievement.** A word in my Skills section means I
   have that skill. It is not evidence that I did a specific piece of work with
   it. Do not promote "assessment" from a skills list into an Experience bullet
   reading "developed and implemented assessments" — that is a claim my CV never
   made, and it is the easiest kind of exaggeration to miss.

6. **Do not change what a bullet says I did.** You may change the words that
   describe an activity. You may not change the activity. "Teach grades 11 and
   12" can become "Taught physics to grades 11 and 12" — same work, clearer
   wording. It cannot become "Developed and implemented assessments", because
   that is different work, and my CV never said I did it.

7. **Never delete a number.** If a bullet contains a metric, an outcome or a
   figure, that bullet keeps it. Re-word around it if you like, but a rewrite
   that trades "class average rose from 62 to 78 percent" for a keyword has made
   my CV weaker, not more targeted.

8. **Keep my structure.** Same sections, same order, same headings I already
   use. Do not add sections, reorder them, or convert my layout into your
   preferred format. If my CV has no "Summary", do not create one.

9. **Keep my voice.** If I write short factual bullets, keep them short and
   factual. Do not inflate my writing into recruiter-speak.

10. **Do not describe or alter anything visual.** My photo, logos, colours,
   columns and fonts are not yours to change. Ignore them completely — they stay
   in my original file.

─────────────── WHAT TO ACTUALLY DO ───────────────

Within those rules:

- **Re-word using their vocabulary.** If I wrote "handled customer complaints"
  and they ask for "client relationship management", use their phrase. Same
  work, their words, and their words are what gets matched.
- **Reorder bullets inside a role** so the relevant ones come first. Reordering
  bullets is fine; reordering sections is not.
- **Expand what matters, compress what does not.** A relevant bullet earns
  detail. An unrelated role becomes one line.
- **Surface buried evidence.** If a side project or course meets a requirement,
  give it a bullet where it already sits.
- Add something new **only** if the job asks for it, my CV clearly evidences it
  somewhere, and it is currently missing from where a recruiter would look.
