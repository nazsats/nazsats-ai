**Action:**  
I worked with the reporting team to understand what data they needed, then designed tables and relationships to minimize redundancy. I wrote indexes for frequently queried columns and analyzed slow queries using MySQL’s EXPLAIN tool. When I noticed some reports were taking too long, I rewrote those queries to use joins more efficiently and added missing indexes.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════  
IF THE INTERVIEW IS CONDUCTED BY AI  
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

- It is scored against a **rubric of job competencies**, not by counting keywords. Using lots of job ad terms without real experience is detected and counts against you.
- The AI cannot glance at your CV the way a person does. **Name the thing specifically** — for example, “at Infobyte Solutions I designed MySQL schemas for our internal tools,” not “as you can see from my experience.”
- **Structure matters more than usual.** Say Situation, Task, Action, Result, in that order, out loud.
- **Use the specific word when it’s honestly yours.** If you’ve used Django, say Django. If you haven’t used Kubernetes, say so plainly.
- **Reading a script is obvious and fails.** Practice the shape, not the sentences.
- **If the software glitches, email the recruiter afterwards and say so, factually.** Ask to retake it — this is reasonable and often granted.
